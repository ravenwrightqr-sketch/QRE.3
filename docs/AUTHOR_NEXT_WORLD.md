# QRE AUTHOR — NEXT WORLD OPERATING MANUAL

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Branch:** `elite-universal-rebuild-v10`

This is the forward operating manual for making the QRE author extraordinary. It is intentionally separate from the historical changelog. `docs/AUTHOR_CHANGELOG.md` records what happened. This file records **what we believe now, what we are trying next, and what must not be forgotten.**

## 1. THE PRODUCT WE ARE BUILDING

QRE is not a novel generator.

QRE is a **rapid-attention experience author** that takes reality, memory, presence, media, and human intent and discovers a sequence people want to keep watching.

The target feeling is:

> **I didn't expect that.**
>
> **Show me the next cut.**

The output may become a wedding memory, a rave chapter, a dog's travel story, a housekeeping receipt, a pool-cleaning memory, a business experience, a horror sequence, an artifact story, or a personal story.

The domain changes. The intelligence does not.

## 2. CANONICAL ARCHITECTURE

```text
PROMPT / MEDIA / MEMORY / PRESENCE / ANALYTICS
                    ↓
             UNIVERSAL COGNITION
                    ↓
      WORLD + ENTITIES + RELATIONSHIPS
                    ↓
       SIGNIFICANCE + HISTORY + LEARNING
                    ↓
      CREATIVE OPERATIONS / COMPETITION
                    ↓
             CHOOSE THE MOVIE
                    ↓
           UNIVERSAL AUTHOR BRAIN
                    ↓
              CUT MOUTH
                    ↓
          CINEMATIC EXPERIENCE
                    ↓
              WORLD MEMORY
```

### Architectural rule

**One Brain. Many specialists. One author. One mouth.**

Pet, groomer, wedding, event, home, etc. may eventually supply domain knowledge, vocabulary, actions, and constraints. They must not become separate cognition systems.

## 3. WHAT THE BRAIN MUST DO

Before language, the Brain should understand:

- who or what is the temporary star
- what is actually known
- what is merely unknown
- what has happened before
- what changed today
- what repeats
- what has history
- what has personality
- what has contradiction
- what has emotional or social weight
- what is visually or conceptually strong
- what can become a callback
- what has already been overused
- what could make the viewer curious
- what kind of continuation would feel earned

Then it should privately compete between **different ways of seeing the same reality**.

Not five paraphrases.

Examples of fundamentally different lenses:

```text
character comedy
status inversion
quiet rivalry
ritual
tiny rebellion
recurring relationship
mystery
understatement
escalation
unexpected tenderness
identity
absurdity
horror through normality
```

The Brain should attack weak candidates:

```text
generic
obvious
repeated
unsupported
too explanatory
predictable
weak payoff
wrong chapter
```

Then it chooses one movie and stays loyal to it.

## 4. CHARACTER GRAVITY

The subject is the temporary star.

The service, business, task, location, event, object, or circumstance is the world the subject experiences.

Character gravity comes from:

```text
attitude
choice
resistance
preference
contradiction
habit
status
callback
consequence
voice
```

It does **not** mean:

```text
Coco
Coco
Coco
Coco
```

Once identity is established, the subject name has low novelty value. It should be reused only when naming the subject itself adds information or creates a deliberate effect.

The subject may remain completely obvious through behavior, attitude, relationship, object interaction, and consequence without repeating the name.

## 5. SUBJECT WORLD VS RELATIONSHIP WORLD

A detected relationship is not automatically a cinematic character.

For a pet world:

```text
Coco world
   ↓
Coco's history
Coco's preferences
Coco's recurring objects
Coco's experiences
Coco's attention
```

The owner's relationship can exist as persistent living memory without becoming a repeated on-screen participant.

Database language such as `owner` is an internal relationship label. The mouth should use a lived relationship only when that relationship is actually interesting and supported.

A second person's appearance is useful when it changes the primary subject's world, not merely because the entity was detected.

## 6. SERVICE-STAGE RULE

A boring job is not boring material.

Housekeeping, pool cleaning, grooming, maintenance, repair, delivery, and other real services are legitimate cinematic raw material.

The author should make the **customer / subject** interesting while keeping the provider in the background unless the source explicitly makes the provider part of the story.

```text
housekeeping → resident / guest becomes the story
pool cleaning → homeowner / property becomes the story
pet grooming → pet becomes the story
wedding service → couple / guest becomes the story
concert service → attendee becomes the story
```

Never manufacture staff dialogue, behavior, personality, or physical actions.

## 7. REALITY IS SACRED

Creative framing is allowed.
Creative facts are not.

Truth can come from:

```text
explicit prompt evidence
user/runtime data
subject-owned memory
presence
media metadata when actually supplied
location/time data when actually supplied
```

The author must never promote its own inference into canonical truth.

### Identity rule

```text
explicitly established
        ↓
canonical subject truth
        ↓
author may use it
```

Unknown means unknown.

This applies to:

- sex
- gender
- pronouns
- relationships
- ownership
- dates
- locations
- people
- physical actions
- outcomes

## 8. CUT GRAMMAR

The author is splicing film.

### Core rule

**ONE LINE = ONE ATTENTION MOMENT.**

A line can be:

```text
2 words
4 words
8 words
12 words
```

Length follows the creative job.

### Compression rule

Do not optimize for fewer words.

Optimize for:

> **the strongest idea with the least explanation.**

### Why the short cuts worked

A line such as:

> **The monster appeared.**

creates an image.

Then:

> **Pink bows everywhere.**

changes the meaning of the first cut and creates another question.

The power is the **gap and implication**, not the word count.

### Forbidden failure pattern

```text
Coco walks in nervous, sees the bow, trembles, then accepts a treat.
```

That is multiple shots disguised as one sentence.

Prefer the ideas to become separate cuts.

### Current mouth constraints

- no paragraph prose
- no comma-stuffed shot lists
- no semicolon-stuffed shots
- no fake camera directions
- no generic cinematic filler
- no automatic theme announcements
- no generic farewell
- no mechanical subject repetition
- no database relationship labels unless they are actually meaningful to the viewer

## 9. ATTENTION ENGINE

Every cut should do at least one of these:

```text
create a question
change an expectation
reveal a character trait
raise pressure
reverse status
widen the world
reframe the previous cut
create a callback
change meaning
pay something off
```

Strong reusable relationships include:

```text
IMAGE → WIDEN
QUESTION → REFRAME
CLAIM → CONTRADICTION
CHARACTER → WORLD RESPONSE
SETUP → CONSEQUENCE
DETAIL → HIDDEN WORLD
NORMAL → VIOLATION
ORDINARY → IMPORTANT
```

These are **creative operations**, not templates.

The author must discover which relationship belongs to the material.

### Attention value

A literal action is not automatically a beat.

```text
Coco barks.
Owner fixes bow.
Owner smiles.
```

These may be true while still being weak cinematic material.

The Brain must ask:

```text
What does the viewer already know?
What genuinely changes here?
Does this add a new implication?
Does it create wanting for the next cut?
```

### Identity versus novelty

Identity is established state.

Attention is new state.

Once the viewer knows who the subject is, repeating the name is usually not a useful attention event.

## 10. LIVING MEMORY

QRE must accumulate a world rather than regenerate isolated stories.

A returning chapter should know:

```text
who this is
what happened before
what detail is recurring
what changed today
what meaning the callback had last time
what meaning it can have now
```

A callback is not repetition.

```text
old detail
   ↓
return
   ↓
new context
   ↓
meaning changes
```

The next chapter should sometimes deliberately break a familiar pattern when the world has become over-dependent on it.

Example principle:

> If bows have carried three chapters, stop using bows as the creative crutch unless today's reality gives them new meaning.

## 11. THE AUTHOR MUST LEARN

Creative learning should accumulate at the world/entity level and author level.

The Brain should remember:

```text
accepted angles
rejected angles
successful lenses
overused motifs
used phrases
novelty pressure
chapter callbacks
subject preferences
```

But learning must not become a phrase blacklist that slowly strangles creativity.

Learning should change **search behavior**, not just wording.

## 12. COGNITIVE SEARCH RULE — NEW CANONICAL

The cognition layer must not behave like a hidden copywriter farm.

We found multiple large hardcoded prose generators that contained:

- stock openings
- stock endings
- object-to-metaphor mappings
- service-specific prose
- genre-specific canned sentences
- canned `creative` strategies that were rewarded by numeric score

That architecture is now considered a **failed creativity mechanism**.

The cognition layer should produce **creative operations and evidence**, not polished prose.

Examples:

```text
subject-gravity
detail-isolation
foreground-background
world-expansion
callback-reframe
surprise-isolation
reversal
consequence
meaning-through-history
comic-reframe
ordinary-to-ominous
tender-understatement
withhold-explanation
specificity-over-generalization
compression
```

The universal author is responsible for turning those operations into original language.

### Why

A canned sentence can look creative in code review while teaching the system to memorize tricks.

The real moat is:

```text
world understanding
+
history
+
significance
+
creative search
+
learning
```

not:

```text
more clever sentence templates
```

## 13. TRUTH CRITIC RULE — NEW CANONICAL

The critic must protect truth **without demanding a fact list**.

Old behavior:

```text
candidate must preserve every detected anchor
```

That creates narration like:

```text
subject + action + object + place + time + detail
```

New behavior:

```text
candidate must remain grounded
candidate must preserve enough explicit evidence to identify what it is about
candidate may deliberately isolate one strong detail
candidate may leave other true facts offscreen
```

A fact does not earn screen time merely by existing.

## 14. EDITORIAL STANDARD

A sequence is not good because:

- it is grammatical
- it is factual
- it has the requested number of beats
- it is short
- it is cinematic-sounding

Those are necessary constraints, not the definition of quality.

The real test is:

```text
CUT 1
↓
I want CUT 2
↓
I want CUT 3
↓
The payoff feels earned
```

### Three quality levels

1. **Semantic validity** — is it true/supportable?
2. **Cinematic movement** — does it actually change what we are watching?
3. **Creative quality** — is it distinctive enough that the viewer would not have predicted it immediately?

Never confuse level 1 with level 3.

## 15. FAST DEVELOPMENT LOOP

The fast loop exists to make experimentation cheap.

```text
ONE MEANINGFUL CHANGE
        ↓
ONE REAL OLLAMA RUN
        ↓
READ THE ACTUAL CUTS
        ↓
DECIDE WHAT BEHAVIOR CHANGED
        ↓
RECORD IT
        ↓
COMMIT
        ↓
NEXT EXPERIMENT
```

### Do not

```text
change 7 things
run 5 tests
forget what changed
argue about the output
```

### Do

```text
change one hypothesis
run one case
inspect the output
learn
repeat
```

## 17. WHAT WE SHOULD BUILD NEXT

### NEXT WORLD 1 — Creative Search Depth

Make internal competition genuinely different.

Do not generate ten stylistic rewrites.

Generate different **interpretations of the world**.

Then score:

```text
specificity
novelty
character fit
continuity
attention potential
payoff potential
```

### NEXT WORLD 2 — Movement Intelligence

Teach the Brain to distinguish:

```text
fact
image
movement
turn
reframe
payoff
```

A fact may be true but still belong nowhere in the sequence.

### NEXT WORLD 3 — Anti-Crutch Intelligence

Track recurring motifs per subject and per world.

The author should know when a successful device has become a crutch.

### NEXT WORLD 4 — Attention State

The Brain should maintain a tiny internal state:

```text
what the viewer knows
what the viewer expects
what remains unresolved
what just changed
what the next cut could do
```

This should guide the sequence without becoming a rigid beat template.

### NEXT WORLD 5 — Creative Memory

Store not only facts but **creative history**:

```text
what angle was used
what motif was used
what callback landed
what was rejected
what the subject tends to do
what the author has already overused
```

### NEXT WORLD 6 — Specialist Intelligence

Only after the universal author is excellent.

Specialists should supply:

```text
domain knowledge
vocabulary
constraints
actions
opportunities
```

They plug into the same Brain.

## 18. WHAT WE MUST NOT DO

Do not solve weak creativity by adding:

```text
more templates
more beat labels
more generic phrase bans
more domain branches
more hardcoded Coco logic
more word-count rules
more regexes
more fixed emotional arcs
more polished canned candidates
```

Those are signs that cognition is compensating for itself instead of getting smarter.

## 19. DEFINITION OF DONE

The author is not done when the tests are green.

The author is done when, across unfamiliar prompts:

```text
ordinary input
      ↓
QRE understands it
      ↓
QRE notices something interesting
      ↓
QRE discovers a specific angle
      ↓
QRE creates clean cuts
      ↓
viewer wants the next cut
      ↓
payoff lands
      ↓
next chapter knows what happened
      ↓
QRE avoids its old tricks
```

And we can look at a boring service like housekeeping or pool cleaning and say:

> **That should have been boring. Somehow it wasn't.**
