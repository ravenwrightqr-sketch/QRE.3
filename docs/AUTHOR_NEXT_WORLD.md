## 1. THE PRODUCT WE ARE BUILDING

QRE is not a novel generator.

QRE is a **universal adaptive experience engine** whose authoring layer can take reality, memory, presence, media, human intent, analytics, and learning and discover a useful, memorable experience that can continue evolving.

The cinematic Author target feeling remains:

> **I didn't expect that.**
>
> **Show me the next cut.**

The resulting experience may become a wedding memory, a rave chapter, an animal travel story, a housekeeping receipt, a pool-cleaning memory, a service record, a business experience, a horror sequence, an artifact story, a product history, an object story, a home knowledge experience, a goal companion, or another authorized realization.

The domain changes. The intelligence does not.

## 2. AUTHORING MODEL

```text
PROMPT / MEDIA / MEMORY / PRESENCE / ANALYTICS / LEARNING
                    ↓
             UNIVERSAL COGNITION
                    ↓
      WORLD + ENTITIES + RELATIONSHIPS
                    ↓
       SIGNIFICANCE + HISTORY + LEARNING
                    ↓
      CREATIVE COMPETITION / ATTACK
                    ↓
             CHOOSE THE MOVIE
                    ↓
              SEQUENCE PLAY
                    ↓
           UNIVERSAL AUTHOR BRAIN
                    ↓
              CUT MOUTH
                    ↓
          CINEMATIC EXPERIENCE
                    ↓
              WORLD MEMORY
```

### Architectural rule

**One Brain. Many specialists. One author. One mouth. One sequence model.**

Pet, groomer, wedding, event, home, object, service, product, or other domains may supply knowledge, vocabulary, actions, and constraints. They must not become separate cognition systems.

## 3. WHAT THE BRAIN MUST DO

Before language, the Brain should understand:

- who or what is the temporary star
- what is actually known
- what is merely unknown
- what has happened before
- what changed today
- what repeats
- what has history
- what has personality or character gravity
- what has contradiction
- what has emotional or social weight
- what is visually or conceptually strong
- what can become a callback
- what has already been overused
- what could make the viewer curious
- what kind of continuation would feel earned

Then it should privately compete between **different ways of seeing the same reality**.

Not five paraphrases.

Examples of fundamentally different lenses:

```text
character comedy
status inversion
quiet rivalry
ritual
tiny rebellion
recurring relationship
mystery
understatement
escalation
unexpected tenderness
identity
absurdity
horror through normality
```

The Brain should attack weak candidates:

```text
generic
obvious
repeated
unsupported
too explanatory
predictable
weak payoff
wrong chapter
```

Then it chooses one movie and stays loyal to it.

## 4. REALITY IS NOT SEQUENCE

Reality answers:

```text
What exists?
What happened?
Who is involved?
What is explicitly known?
What happened before?
```

Sequence Play answers:

```text
What does the viewer know now?
What do they expect next?
What just changed?
What question is alive?
What should the next cut cause?
What can pay off later?
```

A true fact is **not automatically a beat**.

Identity and descriptive facts establish the world upstream. They enter the sequence when they are useful to the dramatic movement.

## 5. SEQUENCE PLAY — HOW THE SHOW ACTUALLY PLAYS

QRE is authoring **a sequence over time**, not generating isolated lines.

The sequence model is a model of **viewer-state movement**.

For each cut, the Brain should reason privately about:

```text
viewerBefore
what the viewer now understands
informationGain
attentionShift
what remains unresolved
nextPromise
payoffConnection
viewerAfter
```

Those fields are control information, not viewer-facing prose.

The author should discover:

```text
how long the sequence needs to be
where to cut
when to withhold
when to reveal
when to reframe
when to accelerate
when to stop
```

There is no permanent beat count. The material earns its number of cuts.

## 6. CHARACTER / SUBJECT GRAVITY

The subject is the temporary star.

The service, business, task, location, event, object, animal, product, or circumstance is the world the subject experiences.

Character gravity comes from:

```text
attitude
choice
resistance
preference
contradiction
habit
status
callback
consequence
voice
history
```

The subject does not need to be a person. The same authoring principle applies to an animal, object, property, product, service, place, or other meaningful subject when the source gives it enough context to support an experience.

## 7. SERVICE / ORDINARY-MATERIAL RULE

A boring job is not boring material.

Housekeeping, pool cleaning, grooming, maintenance, repair, delivery, service visits, records, and other real work are legitimate authoring material.

The Author should discover the interesting meaning already present without inventing staff dialogue, behavior, personality, physical actions, or outcomes.

Examples include:

```text
housekeeping → resident / guest / home becomes the story
pool cleaning → homeowner / property becomes the story
pet grooming → animal becomes the story
wedding service → couple / guest becomes the story
repair → object / owner / transformation becomes the story
service visit → customer / result / relationship becomes the story
```

## 8. REALITY IS SACRED

Creative framing is allowed.
Creative facts are not.

Truth can come from:

```text
explicit prompt evidence
user/runtime data
subject-owned memory
presence
media metadata when actually supplied
location/time data when actually supplied
analytics when it actually records an event
```

The author must never promote its own inference into canonical truth.

Unknown means unknown.

## 9. SOURCE LEDGER

Author input must preserve provenance instead of flattening everything into one bag of strings.

Canonical buckets include:


The purpose is to prevent:

```text

```

## 10. CUT GRAMMAR

The author is splicing film.

### Core rule

**ONE LINE = ONE ATTENTION MOMENT.**

A line can be short or longer when the creative job requires it.

Optimize for:

> **the strongest idea with the least explanation.**

Do not confuse compression with arbitrary word-count rules.

Forbidden failure patterns remain:

```text
paragraph prose
comma-stuffed shot lists
semicolon-stuffed shots
fake camera directions
generic cinematic filler
automatic theme announcements
generic farewells
mechanical subject repetition
invented physical events
invented named participants
unsupported emotional claims
```

## 12. LIVING MEMORY

QRE must accumulate a world rather than regenerate isolated stories.

A returning chapter should know:

```text
who / what this is
what happened before
what detail is recurring
what changed today
what meaning the callback had last time
what meaning it can have now
```

A callback is not repetition:

```text
old detail
   ↓
return
   ↓
new context
   ↓
meaning changes
```

The author should also know when a successful motif has become a crutch.

## 13. THE AUTHOR MUST LEARN

Creative learning should accumulate at the appropriate authorized identity/world, asset, experience, and author scopes.

The Brain should be capable of remembering:

```text
accepted angles
rejected angles
successful lenses
overused motifs
used phrases
novelty pressure
chapter callbacks
subject preferences
behavioral winners

```

Learning should change **search behavior**, not just wording.

Account/organization membership is not permission to merge unrelated identity learning.

## 14. EDITORIAL STANDARD

A sequence is not good because:

- it is grammatical
- it is factual
- it has the requested number of beats
- it is short
- it sounds cinematic

These are constraints, not the definition of quality.

The real test remains:

```text
CUT 1
↓
I want CUT 2
↓
I want CUT 3
↓
The payoff feels earned
```

### Three quality levels

1. **Semantic validity** — is it true/supportable?
2. **Cinematic movement** — does it actually change what we are watching?
3. **Creative quality** — is it distinctive enough that the viewer would not have predicted it immediately?

Never confuse level 1 with level 3.
