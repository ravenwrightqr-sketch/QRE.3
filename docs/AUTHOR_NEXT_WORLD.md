# QRE AUTHOR — NEXT WORLD OPERATING MANUAL

**Status:** ACTIVE
**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Branch:** `elite-universal-rebuild-v10`

This is the forward operating manual for making the QRE author extraordinary. It is intentionally separate from the historical changelog. `docs/AUTHOR_CHANGELOG.md` records what happened. This file records **what we believe now, what we are trying next, and what must not be forgotten.**

## 1. THE PRODUCT WE ARE BUILDING

QRE is not a novel generator.

QRE is a **rapid-attention experience author** that takes reality, memory, presence, media, and human intent and discovers a sequence people want to keep watching.

The target feeling is:

> **I didn't expect that.**
>
> **Show me the next cut.**

The output may become a wedding memory, a rave chapter, a dog's travel story, a housekeeping receipt, a pool-cleaning memory, a business experience, a horror sequence, an artifact story, or a personal story.

The domain changes. The intelligence does not.

## 2. CANONICAL ARCHITECTURE

```text
PROMPT / MEDIA / MEMORY / PRESENCE / ANALYTICS
                    ↓
             UNIVERSAL COGNITION
                    ↓
      WORLD + ENTITIES + RELATIONSHIPS
                    ↓
       SIGNIFICANCE + HISTORY + LEARNING
                    ↓
      CREATIVE COMPETITION / ATTACK
                    ↓
             CHOOSE THE MOVIE
                    ↓
              SEQUENCE PLAY
                    ↓
           UNIVERSAL AUTHOR BRAIN
                    ↓
              CUT MOUTH
                    ↓
          CINEMATIC EXPERIENCE
                    ↓
              WORLD MEMORY
```

### Architectural rule

**One Brain. Many specialists. One author. One mouth. One sequence model.**

Pet, groomer, wedding, event, home, etc. may eventually supply domain knowledge, vocabulary, actions, and constraints. They must not become separate cognition systems.

## 3. WHAT THE BRAIN MUST DO

Before language, the Brain should understand:

- who or what is the temporary star
- what is actually known
- what is merely unknown
- what has happened before
- what changed today
- what repeats
- what has history
- what has personality
- what has contradiction
- what has emotional or social weight
- what is visually or conceptually strong
- what can become a callback
- what has already been overused
- what could make the viewer curious
- what kind of continuation would feel earned

Then it should privately compete between **different ways of seeing the same reality**.

Not five paraphrases.

Examples of fundamentally different lenses:

```text
character comedy
status inversion
quiet rivalry
ritual
tiny rebellion
recurring relationship
mystery
understatement
escalation
unexpected tenderness
identity
absurdity
horror through normality
```

The Brain should attack weak candidates:

```text
generic
obvious
repeated
unsupported
too explanatory
predictable
weak payoff
wrong chapter
```

Then it chooses one movie and stays loyal to it.

## 4. REALITY IS NOT SEQUENCE

This is now canonical.

Reality answers:

```text
What exists?
What happened?
Who is involved?
What is explicitly known?
What happened before?
```

Sequence Play answers:

```text
What does the viewer know now?
What do they expect next?
What just changed?
What question is alive?
What should the next cut cause?
What can pay off later?
```

A true fact is **not automatically a beat**.

Example:

```text
Coco is male.
Coco is a poodle.
Coco loves treats.
```

These may be valuable world facts. They are not three attention cuts merely because they add information.

Identity should live upstream in canonical subject truth. It enters the sequence only when identity itself is the dramatic discovery.

## 5. SEQUENCE PLAY — HOW THE SHOW ACTUALLY PLAYS

QRE is authoring **a sequence over time**, not generating isolated lines.

The sequence model is a model of **viewer-state movement**.

For each cut, the Brain should reason privately about:

```text
viewerBefore
what the viewer now understands
informationGain
attentionShift
what remains unresolved
nextPromise
payoffConnection
viewerAfter
```

But those fields are not the show. They are the **control system behind the show**.

The author should discover the sweet spot of:

```text
how long the sequence needs to be
where to cut
when to withhold
when to reveal
when to reframe
when to accelerate
when to stop
```

There is no permanent 4-beat or 5-beat requirement.

The sequence may earn 2, 3, 4, 7, or more cuts.

### The ideal behavior

```text
CUT 1
→ viewer notices something

CUT 2
→ meaning changes / curiosity rises

CUT 3
→ new implication or escalation

CUT 4
→ payoff / callback / image / consequence
```

The exact pattern must emerge from the material.

## 6. CHARACTER GRAVITY

The subject is the temporary star.

The service, business, task, location, event, object, or circumstance is the world the subject experiences.

Character gravity comes from:

```text
attitude
choice
resistance
preference
contradiction
habit
status
callback
consequence
voice
```

It does **not** mean:

```text
Coco
Coco
Coco
Coco
```

The name can disappear for several cuts while the character remains obvious.

## 7. SERVICE-STAGE RULE

A boring job is not boring material.

Housekeeping, pool cleaning, grooming, maintenance, repair, delivery, and other real services are legitimate cinematic raw material.

The author should make the **customer / subject** interesting while keeping the provider in the background unless the source explicitly makes the provider part of the story.

```text
housekeeping → resident / guest becomes the story
pool cleaning → homeowner / property becomes the story
pet grooming → pet becomes the story
wedding service → couple / guest becomes the story
concert service → attendee becomes the story
```

Never manufacture staff dialogue, behavior, personality, or physical actions.

## 8. REALITY IS SACRED

Creative framing is allowed.
Creative facts are not.

Truth can come from:

```text
explicit prompt evidence
user/runtime data
subject-owned memory
presence
media metadata when actually supplied
location/time data when actually supplied
```

The author must never promote its own inference into canonical truth.

### Identity rule

```text
explicitly established
        ↓
canonical subject truth
        ↓
author may use it
```

Unknown means unknown.

This applies to:

- sex
- gender
- pronouns
- relationships
- ownership
- dates
- locations
- people
- physical actions
- outcomes

## 9. SOURCE LEDGER

Author input must preserve provenance instead of flattening everything into one bag of strings.

Canonical buckets:

```text
IDENTITY
FACTS
SOURCE MOMENTS
MEMORY
TRAJECTORY
PRESENCE
PREFERENCES / CREATIVE LEARNING
```

The purpose is to prevent the author from converting:

```text
creative guidance → fact
inference → fact
context → physical event
role label → cinematic character
```

## 10. CUT GRAMMAR

The author is splicing film.

### Core rule

**ONE LINE = ONE ATTENTION MOMENT.**

A line can be:

```text
2 words
4 words
8 words
12 words
```

Length follows the creative job.

### Compression rule

Do not optimize for fewer words.

Optimize for:

> **the strongest idea with the least explanation.**

### Why the short cuts worked

A line such as:

> **The monster appeared.**

creates an image.

Then:

> **Pink bows everywhere.**

changes the meaning of the first cut and creates another question.

The power is the **gap and implication**, not the word count.

### Forbidden failure pattern

```text
Coco walks in nervous, sees the bow, trembles, then accepts a treat.
```

That is multiple shots disguised as one sentence.

Prefer the ideas to become separate cuts.

### Current mouth constraints

- no paragraph prose
- no comma-stuffed shot lists
- no semicolon-stuffed shots
- no fake camera directions
- no generic cinematic filler
- no automatic theme announcements
- no generic farewell
- no mechanical subject repetition
- no invented physical events
- no invented named participants
- no unsupported emotional claims

## 11. ATTENTION ENGINE

Every cut should do at least one of these:

```text
create a question
change an expectation
reveal a character trait
raise pressure
reverse status
widen the world
reframe the previous cut
create a callback
change meaning
pay something off
```

Strong reusable relationships include:

```text
IMAGE → WIDEN
QUESTION → REFRAME
CLAIM → CONTRADICTION
CHARACTER → WORLD RESPONSE
SETUP → CONSEQUENCE
DETAIL → HIDDEN WORLD
NORMAL → VIOLATION
ORDINARY → IMPORTANT
```

These are **creative operations**, not templates.

The author must discover which relationship belongs to the material.

## 12. LIVING MEMORY

QRE must accumulate a world rather than regenerate isolated stories.

A returning chapter should know:

```text
who this is
what happened before
what detail is recurring
what changed today
what meaning the callback had last time
what meaning it can have now
```

A callback is not repetition.

```text
old detail
   ↓
return
   ↓
new context
   ↓
meaning changes
```

The next chapter should sometimes deliberately break a familiar pattern when the world has become over-dependent on it.

Example principle:

> If bows have carried three chapters, stop using bows as the creative crutch unless today's reality gives them new meaning.

## 13. THE AUTHOR MUST LEARN

Creative learning should accumulate at the world/entity level and author level.

The Brain should remember:

```text
accepted angles
rejected angles
successful lenses
overused motifs
used phrases
novelty pressure
chapter callbacks
subject preferences
```

But learning must not become a phrase blacklist that slowly strangles creativity.

Learning should change **search behavior**, not just wording.

## 14. EDITORIAL STANDARD

A sequence is not good because:

- it is grammatical
- it is factual
- it has the requested number of beats
- it is short
- it is cinematic-sounding

Those are necessary constraints, not the definition of quality.

The real test is:

```text
CUT 1
↓
I want CUT 2
↓
I want CUT 3
↓
The payoff feels earned
```

### Three quality levels

1. **Semantic validity** — is it true/supportable?
2. **Cinematic movement** — does it actually change what we are watching?
3. **Creative quality** — is it distinctive enough that the viewer would not have predicted it immediately?

Never confuse level 1 with level 3.

## 15. FAST DEVELOPMENT LOOP

The fast loop exists to make experimentation cheap.

```text
ONE MEANINGFUL CHANGE
        ↓
ONE REAL OLLAMA RUN
        ↓
READ THE ACTUAL SEQUENCE + CUTS
        ↓
DECIDE WHAT BEHAVIOR CHANGED
        ↓
RECORD IT
        ↓
COMMIT
        ↓
NEXT EXPERIMENT
```

### Do not

```text
change 7 things
run 5 tests
forget what changed
argue about the output
```

### Do

```text
change one hypothesis
run one case
inspect sequence play and cuts
learn
repeat
```

### Level 4 — Memory

```text

```

Tests living continuity and changed-meaning callbacks.

### Level 5 — Sparse input


### Important benchmark separation

`COCO` is the **universal creator baseline**.

Do not use a returning chapter's special continuity requirements to judge whether the universal baseline author is correct.

## 18. WHAT WE MUST NOT DO

Do not solve weak creativity by adding:

```text
more templates

more generic phrase bans
more domain branches
more hardcoded Coco logic
more word-count rules

more fixed emotional arcs
more versioned contracts without a replacement map
```

Those are signs that the cognition/mouth seam or sequence model is weak.

## 19. DEFINITION OF DONE

The author is not done when the tests are green.

The author is done when, across unfamiliar prompts:

```text
ordinary input
      ↓
QRE understands it
      ↓
QRE notices something interesting
      ↓
QRE discovers a specific angle
      ↓
QRE knows how the show should play
      ↓
QRE creates clean cuts
      ↓
viewer wants the next cut
      ↓
payoff lands
      ↓
next chapter knows what happened
      ↓
QRE avoids its old tricks
```

And we can look at a boring service like housekeeping or pool cleaning and say:

> **That should have been boring. Somehow it wasn't.**
